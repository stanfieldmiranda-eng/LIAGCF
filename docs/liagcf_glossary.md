# LIAGCF Glossary

Working definitions for terms used throughout the Lifecycle-Integrated AI Governance Control Framework.

---

**Administrative control**
A governance mechanism that operates through policies, organizational structures, accountability assignments, decision rights, and audit processes. Administrative controls establish the authority and accountability infrastructure within which technical and operational controls function. In the LIAGCF, administrative controls are assigned to the GOVERN and MAP functions and anchored to Strategy & Design, Procurement, and Development phases.

**AI debris**
Residual risks generated by retired AI systems, including unrevoked model access, persistent data exposure, and residual inference capabilities. Term coined by Frimpong (2026) to describe governance failures at the Retirement and Decommissioning lifecycle phase.

**AI system lifecycle**
The full sequence of phases through which an AI system passes from initial conception through retirement. The LIAGCF uses a seven-phase model: Strategy & Design, Data Preparation, Development, Procurement, Deployment, Operations, and Retirement. Governance requirements differ structurally across phases; lifecycle anchoring makes controls contextually actionable.

**Audit traceability**
The property of a governance control that allows its implementation to be independently verified through documentary evidence. The LIAGCF's six-column structure is designed to produce audit traceability at each control: governance intent (Control Objective), framework alignment (AI RMF Function), operational specification (SP 800-53 Control Family), lifecycle context (Lifecycle Phase), and implementation evidence (Governance Artifacts).

**Control category**
The classification of a governance control as Administrative, Technical, or Operational. In the LIAGCF, this taxonomy is applied as the primary organizational structure rather than as a secondary label. The taxonomy has an established pedigree in information security governance (Hong et al., 2003; Yaokumah, 2017).

**Control objective**
A statement of the governance purpose a control is intended to achieve. Control objectives make design intent explicit and auditable, and allow assessors to evaluate whether a control implementation actually addresses the governance problem the control targets.

**Design Science Research (DSR)**
The research methodology used to develop the LIAGCF. DSR provides a structured process for producing purposefully designed artifacts addressing defined organizational problems through iterative cycles of design, demonstration, and assessment. The LIAGCF was developed using the six-stage process model of Peffers et al. (2007) and evaluated using the Framework for Evaluation in Design Science Research (FEDS) established by Venable et al. (2016).

**Governance artifact**
A documentary output that demonstrates control implementation and provides the evidentiary basis for audit. In the LIAGCF, the Governance Artifacts column specifies example artifacts for each control. These are illustrative examples of implementation evidence, not prescriptive requirements.

**GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance)**
The organizational function responsible for implementing governance structures, managing enterprise risk, and maintaining regulatory compliance. The LIAGCF is designed for GRC practitioners operating under NIST SP 800-53 and related federal or enterprise cybersecurity frameworks.

**Lifecycle anchoring**
The assignment of each LIAGCF control to a specific AI system lifecycle phase. Lifecycle anchoring makes SP 800-53 assignments contextually actionable — a control assigned to Operations has different implementation requirements than the same control family assigned to Data Preparation.

**NIST AI RMF**
The NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI 100-1), published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology in 2023. Organizes AI risk management obligations into four core functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE. The LIAGCF maps each of its 16 controls to one AI RMF core function.

**NIST SP 800-53**
NIST Special Publication 800-53, *Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations* (Rev. 5, 2020). Provides the SP 800-53 control families that the LIAGCF uses as its operationalization layer. The fifth edition introduced the Supply Chain Risk Management (SR) control family, directly relevant to AI procurement governance.

**Operationalization**
The translation of governance principles or framework guidance into assignable, verifiable, auditable controls. The LIAGCF's operationalization mechanism is the SP 800-53 control family assignment: each control is mapped to a specific control family that provides the enforcement pathway for the governance objective.

**Operational control**
A governance mechanism that operates through runtime enforcement, monitoring protocols, incident response procedures, access management, and lifecycle management activities. In the LIAGCF, operational controls are assigned to the MEASURE and MANAGE functions and anchored primarily to Operations and Retirement phases.

**Principles-to-practice gap**
The persistent disconnect between high-level AI governance principles and the assignable, verifiable controls that organizations need to implement governance at scale. The LIAGCF is designed to close this gap by providing a lifecycle-integrated, control-categorized governance structure with SP 800-53 operationalization.

**Six-column alignment pathway**
The LIAGCF's primary translation mechanism. The six columns are: Control Category, Control Objective, NIST AI RMF Function, SP 800-53 Control Family, AI Lifecycle Phase, and Example Governance Artifacts. Together, the six columns form a continuous traceability chain from governance intent to implementation evidence.

**Technical control**
A governance mechanism that operates through automated or technological means — monitoring systems, audit logging pipelines, access enforcement mechanisms, bias evaluation tools. In the LIAGCF, technical controls are assigned to the MAP and MEASURE functions and anchored to Data Preparation, Development, and Operations phases.
